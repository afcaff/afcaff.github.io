# AI-Assisted Residential Energy Management

**Undergraduate Research Assistant | UTSA Power Systems Lab**  
**January 2026 – August 2026**

## Overview

During my time as an undergraduate research assistant at UTSA, I worked on a project focused on using AI to help manage residential electricity usage. The idea was to create a system that could understand simple requests from homeowners, like lowering their electricity bill or making their house cooler, and use those requests to help control appliances such as air conditioners and water heaters.

This was my first experience working on an engineering research project outside of my classes. I got to work with real energy consumption data, improve my Python skills, and learn more about how AI could be used in electrical engineering.

## My Contributions

### Energy Management Agent

- Built a prototype AI agent using OpenAI Agent Builder that could interpret natural-language requests about household energy usage.
- Worked on translating requests like "make my house cooler" or "lower my electricity bill" into actions the energy management system could use.
- Tested and troubleshot the agent's logic, including how it handled different inputs and passed information between steps.

### Data Processing and Analysis

- Worked with the Pecan Street dataset, which included several years of minute-by-minute electricity usage data from hundreds of homes.
- Used Python to sort, filter, and organize the data by household and electrical parameters.
- Prepared smaller datasets that could be used for testing and developing the energy management agent.

### Additional Research

- Researched the electrical generation, cooling, and water requirements of large-scale data centers.
- Looked into how linear optimization could be used to plan electrical generation and other infrastructure needs.

## What I Learned

One of the biggest things I learned from this project was that engineering research involves a lot more trial and error than I expected. A good amount of my time was spent organizing data, figuring out how different parts of the system should work together, and troubleshooting things that weren't working the way I intended.

I also became much more comfortable using Python, especially the pandas library, which I had very little experience with before starting the project.

The project ultimately didn't make it to the hardware testing stage, but I still learned a lot from the development process. It was a great opportunity to apply some of what I'd learned in class while picking up new skills along the way.
